How to write a cover letter

  1. Opening: State the role you're applying for and why you're excited about it. Mention something specific about the company that attracted you.
  2. Middle: Highlight 2–3 relevant achievements with numbers where possible (increased X by Y%, launched product serving Z users). Don't just restate your resume — add context and impact.
  3. Closing: Express enthusiasm and ask for the interview. Include a clear call to action.
  4. Sign-off: Use a professional closing (Sincerely, Warm regards) followed by your name.

Cover letter tips that actually work

  • Address it to a named person — "Dear Rahul" beats "Dear Hiring Manager" every time
  • Open with impact: your strongest relevant achievement, not "I am writing to apply"
  • Mirror the job description's language — use the same keywords they use
  • Keep it under 400 words — one page, 3 focused paragraphs
  • End with confidence: "I look forward to discussing" not "I hope you will consider"

Cover letter format for freshers

If you have no work experience, lead with your academic project, internship, or a relevant personal project. Emphasise your skills, eagerness to learn, and any practical experience (hackathons, freelance work, open source contributions). Show that you've researched the company — name a specific product, initiative, or value that resonates with you.

Do I need a cover letter if the job posting doesn't ask for one?

A cover letter that isn't required but is well-written will always help. It shows initiative and effort. Skip it only if the application system has no field for it. When there is a field — even if marked optional — fill it in. Recruiters notice both.

Should my cover letter match my resume design?

Ideally yes — consistent fonts and header style looks professional and shows attention to detail. It signals that you've thought of your application as a cohesive package. Use the same template style in ResumeKit for your resume and this cover letter generator for the letter.
